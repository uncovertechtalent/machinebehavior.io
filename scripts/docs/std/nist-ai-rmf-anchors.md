> Primary documents, operating bodies, related frameworks, named contributors, reference resources for the NIST AI RMF cluster.

## Primary documents

- **NIST AI 100-1: AI Risk Management Framework 1.0** (January 2023). Foundational framework document. Free PDF from NIST.
- **NIST AI 100-1 Playbook** — actionable suggestions per function and sub-category. Living document; updates published periodically. Free.
- **NIST AI 100-1 Roadmap** — NIST's planned future AI work. Updated periodically.
- **NIST AI 100-1 Crosswalks** — published mappings to ISO 27001, ISO 42001, EU AI Act, NIST Privacy Framework, NIST CSF, OECD AI Principles, others. Updated through 2024-2025.
- **NIST AI 600-1: Generative AI Profile** (July 2024). 200+ suggested actions across 12 GenAI risk categories.
- **NIST AI 100-4: Reducing Risks Posed by Synthetic Content** — supplementary guidance on synthetic-content risks.

## Companion NIST publications

- **NIST SP 1270**: A Proposal for Identifying and Managing Bias in AI (2022). Bias-specific work that informs AI RMF.
- **NIST IR 8312**: Four Principles of Explainable AI (2021).
- **NIST SP 800-218**: Secure Software Development Framework (SSDF) (2022). Bridges to secure AI development.
- **NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0** (February 2024). Voluntary cybersecurity framework. NIST AI RMF and CSF use similar function-based structures.
- **NIST Privacy Framework 1.1** (2024 update). Companion to NIST CSF on the privacy axis.

## Operating bodies

- **NIST** — National Institute of Standards and Technology, US Department of Commerce. Publisher of voluntary frameworks across cybersecurity, privacy, AI, cryptography, software security, IoT, more.
- **NIST AI Safety Institute (AISI)** — established within NIST in 2024. Operationalizes AI safety work, particularly for frontier models. Conducts pre-deployment evaluations through voluntary commitments with major AI developers.
- **NIST AI Resource Center (AIRC)** — central NIST AI content hub at airc.nist.gov.

## Adjacent and competing frameworks

- **ISO/IEC 42001:2023** — sibling certifiable AI management system standard.
- **ISO/IEC 23894:2023** — AI risk management guidance (similar scope to NIST AI RMF but ISO format).
- **OECD AI Principles** (2019, updated 2024) — international voluntary principles. NIST AI RMF and ISO 42001 both consistent with OECD principles.
- **UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of AI** (2021) — international voluntary recommendation.
- **Singapore Model AI Governance Framework** (2020, GenAI extension 2024) + **AI Verify** open-source testing toolkit.
- **EU AI Act (Reg 2024/1689)** — regulatory; NIST AI RMF informs but does not satisfy AI Act compliance.
- **UK AI Action Plan / DSIT AI guidance** — voluntary; pro-innovation framing.
- **OWASP LLM Top 10** (2023, 2025 publication Nov 2024) — operational application-security taxonomy.
- **MITRE ATLAS** — adversarial threat landscape for AI systems.
- **MITRE AI Maturity Model** — emerging maturity framing.

## Named contributors and voices

### NIST AI team

- **Reva Schwartz** — co-led AI RMF development; published widely on bias and trustworthy AI.
- **Apostol Vassilev** — NIST AI Safety Institute leadership; AISI's evaluation work.
- **Elham Tabassi** — long-standing NIST AI lead; AI standards work.
- **Various NIST authors** — co-authors on AI RMF 1.0, Playbook, GenAI Profile, supplementary publications.

### AISI

- AISI staff (post-2024 establishment) — public statements on frontier-model evaluation methodology.
- AISI Director rotates with administration; structural continuity through 2026 maintained.

### External contributors (cited in framework development)

- **AI Verify Foundation (Singapore)** — referenced in international harmonization discussions.
- **Various academic AI safety / AI ethics researchers** — comments on draft framework iterations.
- **Industry contributors** — Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, Meta safety teams via voluntary-commitment processes.

### Critical voices

- **Various AI safety researchers** arguing NIST AI RMF is insufficiently rigorous for frontier-model risks.
- **Industry voices** arguing NIST AI RMF GenAI Profile (200+ actions) is operationally burdensome.
- **Cross-jurisdictional voices** noting US-centric framing in some categories.

## Reference resources

- **airc.nist.gov** — NIST AI Resource Center. Central hub for AI RMF, Playbook, crosswalks, supplementary publications.
- **nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework** — AI RMF official page.
- **nist.gov/aisi** — AI Safety Institute page.
- **OECD AI Policy Observatory** (oecd.ai) — international AI policy tracking, including NIST AI RMF references.

## Regulatory and policy context

- **Executive Order 13859** (February 2019) — initial NIST AI standards direction.
- **Executive Order 14110** (October 2023) — broader AI safety direction; rescinded January 2025.
- **Subsequent administration AI policy** through 2025-2026 — varies; NIST work continues with shifting policy emphasis.
- **NDAA provisions** — annual defense authorization acts have included AI safety / NIST AI work funding provisions.
- **State-level AI legislation** (Colorado AI Act 2024, others 2024-2026) often reference NIST AI RMF as preferred framework.
- **Federal agency AI guidance** (Treasury, FTC, FDA, others) increasingly references NIST AI RMF.
