    ADR-0039: The research section uses a report theme, set by the site chrome

    acceptedowner Stefan Coetzeecreated 2026-10-26updated 2026-10-27reviewed 2026-10-26review by 2027-01-245 min readexplanation

    Context

    The research section (/research/: the claims ledger, experiments, objections, evidence, slips, case files, articles, terms and the red team) read like every other reading page: a serif body on the reading surface, mono tables, and dark in a dark system setting. Its readers are CTOs and CEOs, who read consulting reports every week. Since 2026-10-24 the CTO powersuit page positions the work as a complement to McKinsey's AI operating system article, so a finding on this site sits next to that article in the reader's week.

    On 2026-10-26 a script in the browser read the computed styles of four consulting publishers and four research publishers:

    SiteHeadlinesBodyColour
    mckinsey.comBower, weight 300McKinsey Sans 300, 20/32 pxdeep navy, electric blue, light grey page
    bcg.comHenderson BCG Serif 300Henderson Sansdark green, beige page, neon green accent
    hbr.orgTiempos HeadlineTiempos Text, GT Americared
    deloitte.com (Insights)Adelle Sans Condensed 900Adelle Sans Condensedblack, green
    anthropic.comanthropicSansanthropicSans; anthropicSerif loadedivory page, near-black text
    ourworldindata.orgPlayfair DisplayLatonavy text, cream page
    epoch.aiMessina SansMessina Sansdark teal, mint
    metr.orgInstrument SansInstrument Sansteal green

    bain.com answered with a bot check and was not read. The consulting register is a light-weight serif for headlines, a body of 18 to 20 px, a light page, one deep colour and one accent, and numbered exhibits with a title above and a source line below. Bower is McKinsey's own typeface (Radim Peško with Wolff Olins); McKinsey's own slides fall back to Georgia and Arial.

    Three registers were built on the abliteration experiment page and compared on screenshots: a report (the consulting register), a paper (LaTeX and Tufte: EB Garamond, numbered sections, three-rule tables) and a lab (the research labs: Inter Tight on ivory, mono tables). The paper is closest to the current serif pages and so changes least; the lab reads as an AI lab, not an adviser. Stefan Coetzee chose the report on 2026-10-26, and on 2026-10-27 the paper instead (ADR-0047).

    Decision

    The chrome sets the theme by URL. THEMED in scripts/site_chrome.py becomes a list of URL prefixes and themes: /inside/ gets intranet (ADR-0035), /research/ gets report, every other page gets no attribute. ADR-0047 renames the value to paper. python3 scripts/site_chrome.py --check holds it in the required job, and a new research page gets the theme from the chrome with no step of its own. The theme is fixed: there is no switch for the reader.

    Light in both system settings. The dark values in design/tokens.css skip the report theme, as they skip the intranet theme. Every consulting and research publisher read above serves a light page. The rest of the public site keeps light and dark from the visitor's setting, and the top bar stays dark. Replaced by ADR-0042: every page is light.

    Our colours, their register. The tokens keep the site's teal as the one accent (#1F6B5E on white) and use a deep teal ink (#0F1E22) and a dark teal title band (#0E2A2B). No other publisher's colour or typeface is used, and nothing on the pages claims an affiliation. Replaced by ADR-0047: cream paper, the teal darkened, no band.

    Type. Headlines are Newsreader at weight 300 (Production Type), the body and tables Inter (Rasmus Andersson) at 400, 400 italic and 600, both from @fontsource 5.3.0, latin subset, under the SIL Open Font License 1.1, self-hosted in /fonts/ with their licence files (ADR-0010). The five files weigh about 119 KB together; a browser fetches them only on a page that uses them, so pages outside the theme load nothing new. The chrome (bar, sidebar, footer) keeps its own fonts. Replaced by ADR-0047: EB Garamond throughout; Newsreader and Inter left /fonts/.

    Layout. The eyebrow, title and subtitle sit on the dark band. The band and its light text share one selector, header:has(> h1), so a browser without :has() (Firefox before 121, Safari before 15.4) shows the title in the page colours with no band, never light text on white; h2 headings are Newsreader 300 with a short teal rule above; tables lose their vertical rules and get a dark top rule, uppercase column heads and hairlines between rows. Replaced by ADR-0047: the title is centred with no band, the sections are numbered, tables carry three rules.

    Exhibits. A table with a <caption> is an exhibit: the theme numbers it ("Exhibit 1", "Exhibit 2" in page order) above its caption. Replaced by ADR-0040 for the numbering: the site chrome now writes the number into the HTML. ADR-0047 changes the word: a captioned table is "Table N". Report pages caption their tables: the experiments, the experiment pages, the red-team method, the case files and the articles. Ledgers and indexes (the claims ledger, objections, evidence, slips, the red-team catalogue) keep plain tables. A <p class="exhibit-source"> under an exhibit names the published data behind it and links the file; an exhibit whose data is not published has no source line rather than an unchecked one. In this change the abliteration page has source lines; the other exhibits get them when their run records are published.

    Contrast. On white the text tokens reach 17.1:1, 10.0:1 and 6.9:1 and the teal 6.3:1; on the sunken surface the lowest pair is the teal at 5.7:1. On the band the title reaches 15.2:1, the subtitle 11.6:1 and the eyebrow 8.6:1. The focus ring of a link on the band takes the eyebrow colour (8.6:1), because the teal ring of the rest of the page would reach only 2.4:1 there. Every pair meets WCAG 2.2 AA, the ring the 3:1 of SC 1.4.11. Replaced by ADR-0047 for the paper tokens.

    Consequences

    The research section reads as a set of reports, distinct from the rest of the public site and from Inside, in both system settings. Fifteen pages change by one attribute on <html>; five pages gain table captions, and the abliteration page gains two source lines. A new table on a report page needs a caption to become an exhibit. Readers who set a dark system theme see the research pages light. The social preview images of the research pages still show the old look until they are taken again.
