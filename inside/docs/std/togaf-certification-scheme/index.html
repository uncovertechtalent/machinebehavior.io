    TOGAF Certification Scheme

    owner Stefan Coetzeecreated 2026-05-12updated 2026-06-08not reviewed4 min readreference
    Vault note, not reviewed against the source. Written in the knowledge vault on 2026-05-12 by models working with Stefan Coetzee and published as it stands, with private addresses, e-mail addresses and an employer name redacted. Check claims against the primary source before relying on them.

    How TOGAF certification works. The 10th Edition restructured the scheme into Foundation (Part 1) and Practitioner (Part 2) plus newer modular badges; the experience-based Open CA program sits alongside as the more credible capability signal. Administered by The Open Group via accredited exam providers.

    TOGAF Standard, 10th Edition certifications

    Foundation (Part 1)

    Tests knowledge of TOGAF: terminology, structure, the ADM phases and their objectives, the content framework, the Enterprise Continuum, the Architecture Capability Framework, core concepts.

    • Exam: multiple-choice, ~40 questions, ~60 minutes, ~60% pass mark, closed-book. Available via Pearson VUE and online proctoring.
    • Prerequisites: none.
    • What it validates: that the holder knows TOGAF vocabulary and structure. It does not validate ability to apply TOGAF.
    • Reputation: widely regarded as a memorization exam. Useful as a vocabulary-alignment baseline; weak as a capability signal.

    Practitioner (Part 2)

    Tests ability to apply TOGAF: scenario-based questions where the candidate must select the best response given a context.

    • Exam: scenario-based "gradient" questions (best answer scores most, plausible-but-worse answers score less, wrong answers score zero), ~8 questions, ~90 minutes, open-book (the TOGAF Standard is available during the exam).
    • Prerequisites: Foundation (Part 1) must be passed first, or taken in a combined exam.
    • What it validates: that the holder can reason about applying TOGAF in context. Stronger signal than Foundation, though still exam-based rather than experience-based.

    TOGAF Enterprise Architecture Practitioner

    The combined credential — holding both Part 1 and Part 2. This is the "full" TOGAF certification most job postings mean when they say "TOGAF certified."

    Modular badges (10th Edition additions)

    The 10th Edition restructure introduced (and continues to add) topic-specific badges aligned with Series Guides:

    • TOGAF Business Architecture badges
    • TOGAF Digital Enterprise Architecture badges
    • Others as the Series Guide library expands

    These let practitioners certify in specific TOGAF application areas beyond the general Foundation+Practitioner.

    Bridge certifications

    When a new edition is released, The Open Group provides bridge exams for holders of the prior edition's certification (e.g., TOGAF 9.2 certified → TOGAF 10 via a bridge exam) so existing certifications can be carried forward without retaking the full exam.

    Open CA (Open Certified Architect)

    A distinct program — experience-based, not exam-based. The Open Group's certification for practising architects, recognizing demonstrated experience and skill rather than exam knowledge.

    • Process: the candidate prepares a detailed certification package (experience profile, project descriptions, demonstration of skills against the conformance requirements) and is reviewed by a board of certified architects. Includes a board interview.
    • Levels:
      • Certified Architect — demonstrated experience leading architecture work.
      • Master Certified Architect — broader and deeper experience, leading significant architecture engagements.
      • Distinguished Certified Architect — industry-recognized architecture leadership; subcategories (Chief / Enterprise / Distinguished).
    • Scope: broader than TOGAF. Open CA recognizes architecture practitioner capability generally, regardless of which framework(s) the architect uses. It is the more credible "this person can actually do architecture" signal compared with TOGAF Foundation / Practitioner exams.
    • Adjacent: Open CITS (Open Certified IT Specialist) — the equivalent for IT specialists rather than architects.

    Many large IT consultancies and enterprises run internal certification programs accredited against Open CA, so their architects' internal grades map to the external Open CA levels.

    Accredited training

    The Open Group accredits training organizations to deliver TOGAF courses. Common providers:

    • Good e-Learning / GoGood
    • Architecting the Enterprise / Conexiam
    • Van Haren / Van Haren Learning Solutions
    • Learning Tree International
    • QA Ltd (UK)
    • Simplilearn, Knowledgehut, Edureka (volume online providers)
    • Many regional and consultancy-internal accredited providers

    Self-study + direct exam booking via Pearson VUE is also widely used, particularly for Foundation. The TOGAF Standard documentation is available free for personal use, so self-study is viable.

    Cost orientation

    Indicative (varies by region and provider):

    • Foundation exam only: ~€/$ 360-450.
    • Practitioner exam only: ~€/$ 360-450.
    • Combined Part 1 + Part 2 exam: ~€/$ 550-650.
    • Accredited training (instructor-led, 3-5 days, includes exam vouchers): ~€/$ 1,500-3,000.
    • Self-study materials: ~€/$ 50-300.
    • Open CA: application fees plus the substantial unpaid effort of preparing the certification package (often weeks of work); some employers cover and support this.

    Certification value assessment

    • Foundation: procurement-recognized, low signal value. Worth holding if doing EA-adjacent enterprise work because RFPs ask for it; not a meaningful capability indicator.
    • Practitioner: better signal than Foundation; demonstrates applied reasoning. The combined Foundation+Practitioner is what "TOGAF certified" usually means.
    • Modular badges: useful for demonstrating focus in a specific area (business architecture, digital EA).
    • Open CA: the credible capability signal. Experience-based, board-reviewed, framework-agnostic. If architecture is a primary professional identity, Open CA carries more weight than any TOGAF exam.
    • For SRE / platform-track work: TOGAF Foundation is a reasonable "I speak EA" baseline. Higher TOGAF certifications have diminishing returns vs hands-on platform / reliability work. Open CA is relevant if moving toward an enterprise / principal architect identity.

    SRE and AI-system fit notes

    • No AI-specific TOGAF certification yet. The 10th Edition's digital-EA and Series-Guide-aligned badges touch adjacent ground; there is no "TOGAF for AI architecture" credential. ISO/IEC 42001 (AI management system) lead-implementer / lead-auditor certifications are the nearest AI-governance credentials.
    • Open CA packages can include AI-system architecture work. When preparing an Open CA package, AI-system architecture engagements count as demonstrated experience — designing AI capabilities into an enterprise, governing AI-system risk, leading AI-platform architecture.
    • The certification-mill critique applies. TOGAF Foundation's memorization-exam nature means certificate-counting is a weak hiring / procurement signal for AI-system architecture capability. Look for demonstrated work, not certificate counts.
