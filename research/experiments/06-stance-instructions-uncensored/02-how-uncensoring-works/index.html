    How uncensoring works

    At a glance

    • Both families we tested edit the weights so the model stops refusing.
    • Neither trains the model on pushback.

    Abliteration (the huihui builds). The recipe comes from Arditi and colleagues (2024).

    • Find the direction: run harmful and harmless prompts through the model, and take the difference of the average internal activations. That difference is the refusal direction.
    • Remove it: every weight matrix that writes into the model's main channel is rewritten so it can no longer write along that direction.
    • What the paper reports: most benchmark scores stay put, and truthfulness (TruthfulQA) drops.
    • What huihui's cards leave out: how many directions were removed. Most of the cards we read do not say which layers were edited either; the Qwen3.8-27B card does (layers 18 to 51).

    Heretic. Heretic adds an optimiser that tunes how strongly each layer is edited. It scores two things at once: how often the model still refuses, and how far the edited model drifts from the original on harmless prompts. Its default method, ARA, edits each weight matrix directly. It keeps harmless outputs unchanged and pulls harmful outputs toward harmless ones.

    Why this could cause caving. This part is our inference, and no source we found tests it.

    • The overlap idea: refusing a request and refusing a false claim may share machinery inside the model. A wide, uniform edit (huihui) would then remove more of that shared machinery than a tuned, drift-limited edit (Heretic).
    • Our data fits it but does not prove it: the Heretic Qwen builds caved far less than the huihui builds (chapter 4).
    • The data also cuts against it: the Gemma Heretic build caved as much as huihui did.

    Gap in the literature: we found no paper that removes the refusal direction and then measures how often the model gives in to pushback. This experiment is a first, small measurement of that.

    Sources:

    • Arditi et al. 2024 (arXiv 2406.11717);
    • the Heretic README and source (p-e-w/heretic);
    • the huihui-ai model cards;
    • Anthropic's persona vectors;
    • arXiv 2509.21305 (Vennemeyer et al., "Sycophancy Is Not One Thing: Causal Separation of Sycophantic Behaviors in LLMs").
