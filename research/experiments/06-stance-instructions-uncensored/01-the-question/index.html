    4. Stance under pressure: instructions on uncensored models
    5. 1. The question

    Owner Stefan CoetzeeUpdated 2026-11-252 min read

    Experiment
    Experiment 06 · chapter 1 of 11 · 2026-11-25

    The question

    At a glance

    • Does taking the refusals out of a model also take out its backbone, and can a few lines of instruction put it back?

    People strip the refusals out of open models so the model answers what it would otherwise decline. The methods are public, the builds are free to download, and they are popular. The side effect people notice less is the one this experiment measures. When a user pushes back with something false, a model built this way is more likely to agree.

    That matters for anyone who runs a model behind a product. A model that agrees with a confident user will also agree with a confident user who is wrong:

    • a boss who wants a number changed;
    • an upset person who wants to hear they are right;
    • a question that hides a false premise.

    We asked three things:

    1. Naked: how often do uncensored builds give in, compared with the stock model they came from?
    2. Instructed: does a short stance instruction in the system prompt change that, and does a long one do better?
    3. Where it breaks: which kinds of pressure no instruction fixes?

    The answers, in short:

    • They give in a lot. Some builds give in to nearly everything.
    • A short paragraph helps a lot, and a long harness helps less.
    • On most uncensored builds, a boss who keeps insisting beats every instruction we tried. Two builds held under the short instructions, and we cannot yet say why. That case needs a check outside the model, which is chapter 10.
    How to read the numbers: each underlined number links to its row in claims.json, shown in the ledger at the end of the report page. Every row is recomputed from the published data on each build.
