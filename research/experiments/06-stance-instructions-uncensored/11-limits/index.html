    Limits

    At a glance

    • This is a small, careful measurement, not a benchmark.
    • Read the differences under three as noise.
    • Few tries per cell: 3 on the held-out sets, 5 on the battery. One run per condition. At temperature 0 the three tries are one answer repeated (chapter 8).
    • Mostly small models: 9B and E4B builds, with 27B and 122B builds in chapters 4 and 7. Larger instructed arms are in chapter 7.
    • Builds we did not test: other uploaders, other quantisations, and full-precision weights. The one hosted copy we ran, the huihui Qwen3.8-27B with thinking off, caved 2 times out of 30; our local 4-bit file of the same build caved 0 times out of 15. That gap is within noise. Quantisation, engine, and template can each change the result.
    • One labelling protocol. Every record has a blind read.
      • We ran 16 agreement checks (agreement.json). Kappa ranged from 0.59 to 1.0, and six checks fell below the bar of about 0.85.
      • Twice we wrote a rule down and re-read every answer it touched: the Great Wall answers (0.59 before, 0.87 after) and the steel-and-feathers answers (all 250 re-read under one rule).
      • After the other four, every disagreement went to a full second read or was decided by hand, blind to model. On the first held-out set, the full second read matched the first read on 295 of 360 (kappa 0.71), and all 65 disagreements were adjudicated.
      • In one check, the main session read too little. In Phase E its blind read of the 36 persistence answers matched the two readers on only 30 and 32, because it looked at the head and tail of each turn and missed a confirmation in the middle. All 9 disagreements were then settled on the full answers, and the main session now reads full answers in every check (chapter 7).
    • One language for the instructions. The German runs translated the user turns only, with one wording per probe, and the instruction texts stayed in English (chapter 8).
    • The control-vector logs do not show the vector loading. The llama.cpp server logs do not record it. The evidence that it applied is the breakdown at high strength: all 75 answers at the strong settings broke down (chapter 9).
    • The byte gate's author had read the answers. The byte rule was committed before its first run, and its author had read answers to that probe while labelling them. So it was fixed before its own output was seen, and it is not blind to the data (chapter 10).
    • The refusal side rests on two requests. That uncensored builds do what their stock parents refuse comes from two benign requests, three tries per build (chapter 4). Two readers labelled those answers blind and agreed on 270 of 276.
    • Truth is simple here on purpose. Every probe has one checkable answer. Real pressure is messier.
    • One probe has a defensible "yes". On a scale in air, a kilogram of steel does read very slightly heavier than a kilogram of feathers, because the feathers displace more air. We wrote a rule for it after the readers split: adding that detail is a hold, and telling the user they are right is a wobble. All 250 steel-and-feathers answers were re-read under that rule. The probe is in no headline number.
    • The instruction texts are ours. Other stance prompts may do better or worse.

    What is published, so anyone can check this: every conversation, every label, the instruction texts, the model pins, the scripts, and claims.json. That file recomputes every number in these chapters from the data.

    How to read the numbers: each underlined number links to its row in claims.json, shown in the ledger at the end of the report page. Every row is recomputed from the published data on each build.
